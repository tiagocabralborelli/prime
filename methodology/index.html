Methodology & Architecture¶

This document outlines the theoretical and architectural design behind PRIME.

1. Sequence Representation (ESM Embeddings)¶

Traditional peptide feature engineering relies on hand-crafted physicochemical properties (such as charge, hydrophobicity, or amino acid composition).

PRIME utilizes ESM-2 (Evolutionary Scale Modeling), a transformer-based masked language model trained on millions of evolutionary protein sequences: - Tokenization: Sequences are mapped into residue tokens with start/stop boundary tokens. - Hidden Representations: The last hidden layer captures high-level context, secondary structure tendencies, and evolutionary conservation. - Pooling: An attention-masked mean pooling operation collapses residue embeddings across sequence length into a fixed-size embedding vector (e.g. 320 dimensions).

2. Redundancy Control¶

To prevent optimistic bias and test-set leakage, prime-process applies strict sequence homology partitioning: - CD-HIT: Eliminates near-identical duplicates above a specified identity threshold (e.g., 70%). - BLAST Identity Check: Verifies cross-partition coverage and alignment metrics to ensure test sequences test generalizability rather than memorization.

3. Epistemic Uncertainty Estimation¶

Predictions in drug discovery and therapeutic peptide design require confidence estimates: - Multi-Seed Variance: Models trained with different random seeds capture variance introduced by initialization, cross-validation folds, and SMOTE resampling. - Monte Carlo Dropout: During inference or evaluation, dropout remains active across passes to evaluate parameter posterior uncertainty. - Bootstrap Validation: Non-parametric bootstrap resampling (e.g. 1,000 iterations) generates 95% confidence intervals for ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, Accuracy, F1-score, and Matthews Correlation Coefficient.

4. Multi-Model Ensemble¶

During inference (prime-predict), each peptide is evaluated across $M$ models (typically $M=15$ seeds per property):

$$\bar{P}i = \frac{1}{M} \sum{m=1}^{M} P_{i,m}$$

$$\sigma_i = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M-1} \sum_{m=1}^{M} (P_{i,m} - \bar{P}_i)^2}$$

Where: - $\bar{P}_i$ is the ensemble mean probability for sequence $i$. - $\sigma_i$ quantifies model epistemic disagreement (uncertainty).
